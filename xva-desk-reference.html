xVA field reference

One trade. Five desks. Five different questions about the same number.

A derivative's risk-free value is only the starting point. Between quote and booking, that value gets adjusted by a chain of desks — each one pricing a different friction: counterparty default, your own default, the cost of funding the position, the cost of posting margin, and the capital it ties up. This is a working map of what each desk actually computes, how it computes it, and where it sits in the regulatory chain.

Formulas and mechanics below reflect standard industry xVA practice (as codified across ISDA, BCBS-IOSCO and CRR/Basel III literature), not any single text — treat this as a refresher and cross-reference against your primary sources for derivations and edge cases.

01

CVA desk

Owns counterparty default risk on the bank's derivatives book. Sits between the trading desk and the credit hedging market (single-name CDS, index CDS), and is usually the desk that quotes the counterparty risk charge back into a client price.

Discretized unilateral CVA
CVA ≈ (1 − RC) · Σᵢ EE(tᵢ) · PD(tᵢ₋₁, tᵢ) · DF(tᵢ)

R_C is assumed counterparty recovery (commonly 40% for senior unsecured), EE(t) is expected exposure at time t under the risk-neutral measure, PD is marginal default probability over each interval bootstrapped from CDS spreads (or a proxy — sector/rating curve — if the name is illiquid), and DF discounts to today.

  • Exposure, not value. EE(t) = 𝔼[max(V(t),0)] — CVA only cares about scenarios where the trade is an asset to the bank, because that's the only state where the counterparty's default costs money.
  • Wrong-way risk. When exposure and counterparty credit quality are correlated (e.g. a commodity producer trading a swap that gets more in-the-money exactly as its own credit deteriorates), unilateral CVA understates risk — this needs a joint simulation or an add-on.
  • Netting and collateral collapse EE dramatically — the desk prices the netting set, not the trade in isolation, and a CSA with tight thresholds can cut CVA by an order of magnitude.
Regulatory tie
Basel III's CVA capital charge (and its FRTB-CVA overhaul — BA-CVA for simpler books, SA-CVA for banks with regulatory approval to model CVA sensitivities) sits directly downstream of this desk's exposure engine. SA-CVA effectively asks the desk to compute CVA sensitivities to credit and market risk factors and capitalize them like a trading book.
Expected exposure profile for an amortizing IRS: rises as fixed/floating rates diverge, peaks mid-life, then decays as remaining cashflows shrink toward maturity — the classic "hump" the CVA desk integrates under.

02

Funding (FVA) desk

Sits between the trading desk and Treasury. Prices the reality that hedging an uncollateralized derivative requires the bank to fund variation on the hedge leg at its own unsecured borrowing rate — a cost (or benefit) that has nothing to do with the counterparty's credit.

Funding cost / benefit adjustment
FCA ≈ Σᵢ EPE(tᵢ) · sF(tᵢ) · Δtᵢ · DF(tᵢ)
FBA ≈ Σᵢ ENE(tᵢ) · sF(tᵢ) · Δtᵢ · DF(tᵢ)
FVA = FCA − FBA

s_F is the bank's own unsecured funding spread over the risk-free/OIS curve. EPE (expected positive exposure) drives the cost leg — money the desk must borrow to fund the hedge; ENE (expected negative exposure) drives the benefit leg — money the desk gets to invest/fund itself with.

  • The DVA/FBA overlap problem. FBA and DVA are built off similar intuition (the bank's own credit), and there's an unresolved industry debate — captured in the "FVA controversy" — about whether including both double-counts the bank's own default risk. Most dealers today price FVA using their own funding curve and treat DVA as a separate, often unhedgeable, accounting entry.
  • Collateralized trades funded via CSA reference the CSA rate (often OIS/SOFR), not unsecured funding — so FVA is close to zero on a well-collateralized netting set and material on uncollateralized corporate business.
  • Desk typically expresses funding cost as a term structure — s_F(t) — sourced from the bank's own bond/CDS curve or internal funds transfer pricing (FTP) desk.
Regulatory tie
FVA isn't itself a Basel capital charge — it's a fair value accounting adjustment (IFRS 13 "market participant" exit price) — but EU/UK prudential valuation rules (EBA's Additional Valuation Adjustments, AVA) require an extra capital deduction if a bank can't evidence a defensible FVA methodology, so the desk's documentation matters as much as the number.
EPE above the axis drives FCA (a cost); ENE below drives FBA (a benefit). Net FVA is the asymmetry between the two areas, weighted by the bank's own funding spread.

03

Collateral (ColVA) desk

Often embedded inside the funding or CSA-management function rather than standing alone. Prices the optionality written into every CSA: which currency collateral is posted in, whether it can be substituted, and how the remuneration rate compares to the "clean" OIS discounting assumption.

ColVA — value of CSA terms vs. a "perfect" CSA
ColVA ≈ Σᵢ C(tᵢ) · [ ractual(tᵢ) − rOIS(tᵢ) ] · Δtᵢ · DF(tᵢ)

Where C(t) is expected posted collateral and r_actual is the rate the CSA actually pays on collateral (which can differ from clean OIS if the CSA allows multiple eligible currencies, has a non-standard remuneration rate, or embeds thresholds/minimum transfer amounts that leave residual uncollateralized exposure).

  • Cheapest-to-deliver optionality. A CSA that allows collateral in several currencies is effectively a swaption on funding cost — the poster will always deliver whichever currency is cheapest for them, which is a cost to the receiver.
  • Threshold / MTA residual risk means even a "collateralized" netting set carries some uncollateralized exposure below the threshold — this residual still needs a (smaller) CVA/FVA calculation layered on top.
  • This is the desk most directly responsible for negotiating CSA terms with new counterparties — legal (ISDA/CSA) input and quant pricing are unusually tightly coupled here.
Regulatory tie
Indirectly regulatory: BCBS-IOSCO's uncleared margin rules (UMR) dictate who must post variation and initial margin at all, which determines whether a ColVA calculation is even relevant for a given counterparty pair, while ISDA's Standard CSA (SCSA) was itself designed to strip out most of the cheapest-to-deliver optionality that ColVA prices.
ColVA is the price of the gap between the two boxes — every deviation from the "perfect" CSA is optionality handed to the collateral poster.
04

Margin (MVA) desk

A post-2016 addition, born directly out of uncleared margin rules. Prices the funding cost of initial margin (IM) — collateral that's segregated and can't be rehypothecated, so unlike variation margin it's a pure funding drag with no offsetting benefit.

Initial margin funding cost
MVA ≈ Σᵢ IM(tᵢ) · sF(tᵢ) · Δtᵢ · DF(tᵢ)

Structurally identical to FCA, but the exposure driver is IM(t) — modeled initial margin, not expected exposure — and there's no benefit leg to net against, because segregated IM can't be reused to fund anything else.

  • IM ≠ EE. Under ISDA's SIMM (Standard Initial Margin Model), IM is a sensitivity-based VaR proxy (roughly a 99% / 10-day measure), so the desk needs a full SIMM engine (or a fast proxy) projected forward — a materially harder simulation problem than EE.
  • Path dependency. Because SIMM IM depends on the live risk sensitivities of the whole netting set (not just its MTM), MVA is one of the most compute-intensive xVAs to project, often requiring nested Monte Carlo or machine-learning proxies in practice.
  • Largest for directional, long-dated, uncleared trades where forward-starting sensitivities stay elevated for years — this is precisely the profile UMR was designed to discourage.
Regulatory tie
Directly downstream of BCBS-IOSCO's uncleared margin rules (phased in 2016–2022 across six waves by counterparty AANA), which mandated bilateral exchange of initial margin using SIMM or a regulatory schedule — MVA exists as a desk because this regulation exists.
Projected SIMM initial margin over the netting set's life. Every dollar under this curve is funded at the bank's unsecured spread with nothing netting against it.
05

Capital (KVA) desk

The newest and most contested of the five. Prices the cost of the regulatory capital the trade consumes over its life — treating the bank's target return on equity as a genuine cost of doing the trade, the way FVA treats funding.

Cost-of-capital adjustment
KVA ≈ Σᵢ RC(tᵢ) · (h − rf) · Δtᵢ · DF(tᵢ)

RC(t) is projected regulatory capital (RWA × capital ratio) consumed by the trade — driven mainly by counterparty credit risk RWA (via SA-CCR exposure-at-default) plus the CVA capital charge itself. h is the bank's hurdle rate / target ROE, and r_f the risk-free rate — the spread between them is what shareholders actually demand for tying up equity.

  • KVA is capital charging capital. Because RC(t) includes the CVA capital charge, KVA is partly "the cost of capitalizing the CVA desk's own risk" — a recursive relationship that makes clean attribution genuinely hard.
  • No universal accounting standard. Unlike CVA/FVA, KVA often isn't taken through fair value — many banks track it as an internal pricing/profitability overlay (feeding return-on-capital-at-risk metrics) rather than a balance-sheet adjustment, so its treatment varies materially by institution.
  • SA-CCR sensitivity. Because SA-CCR (which replaced the Current Exposure Method) calculates exposure-at-default using supervisory add-ons per asset class, KVA is highly sensitive to netting set composition — diversifying across asset classes inside one CSA can materially reduce it.
Regulatory tie
Directly built on the Basel III/CRR2 (and Basel 3.1 / CRR3 finalization) capital framework: SA-CCR for counterparty credit risk RWA, plus SA-CVA/BA-CVA for the CVA capital add-on — KVA is, in effect, the desk that translates the entire prudential rulebook into a single price.
Capital consumption tracks the exposure profile's shape but is re-weighted by SA-CCR add-ons — capital-heavy asset classes (e.g. long-dated FX, commodities) show a fatter curve than their raw MTM exposure would suggest.

How the five desks actually interact on one netting set

Each desk prices its own friction independently, but they're not additive in a naive sense — collateral terms set by the ColVA/CSA function change the exposure the CVA desk sees; whether a trade is collateralized at all determines whether FVA and MVA are material or near-zero; and the CVA capital charge itself becomes an input to KVA. A single quoted xVA charge to a client is the output of this chain, not a sum of independently-run models.

Regulatory map, desk by desk

CVA — Basel III / CRR2 / FRTB-CVA

Own capital charge since Basel III; overhauled under FRTB-CVA into BA-CVA (basic, formula-driven) and SA-CVA (sensitivities-based, requires supervisory approval). Timelines have varied by jurisdiction — check current EU CRR3 / UK / US implementation dates rather than assuming a fixed date.

FVA — accounting, not capital

Governed by IFRS 13 fair value hierarchy; EU/UK prudential valuation (AVA) rules require a capital deduction if the FVA methodology isn't robustly evidenced. No dedicated Basel capital charge exists for FVA itself.

ColVA — UMR + ISDA SCSA

Shaped by BCBS-IOSCO uncleared margin rules (which counterparties must post at all) and ISDA's Standard CSA, which was explicitly designed to reduce the optionality ColVA prices.

MVA — BCBS-IOSCO UMR / SIMM

Exists because of the phased uncleared margin rules (2016–2022 waves by AANA threshold); the IM projection engine is typically built around ISDA's SIMM methodology.

KVA — SA-CCR + Basel III/CRR3 capital stack

Built on SA-CCR (the standardized approach to counterparty credit risk exposure, replacing CEM) for RWA, plus the CVA capital charge itself as a secondary input — meaning KVA moves whenever either underlying framework is recalibrated. Given the pace of Basel 3.1/CRR3 phase-ins across jurisdictions, treat specific ratios and dates as things to verify against current supervisory text rather than fixed reference points.
