01Company Overview

Incorporated in 1997 and headquartered in Hsinchu City, Taiwan, ChipMOS Technologies Inc. is an independent provider of outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) services, specializing in memory (DRAM, NAND/NOR Flash, SRAM), display driver ICs (DDIC) for LCD/OLED panels, mixed-signal/logic ICs, and MEMS devices.

ChipMOS operates across five reporting segments — Testing, Assembly, Display Panel Driver Semiconductor Assembly & Testing, Bumping, and Others — serving fabless semiconductor companies, integrated device manufacturers (IDMs) and foundries worldwide. The company trades as an ADS on NASDAQ (IMOS) and as common shares on the Taiwan Stock Exchange (8150, listed April 2014), with roughly 5,700 employees across its Taiwan manufacturing base and additional operations touching Japan and China.

02History & Evolution
1997

Incorporation in Hsinchu

ChipMOS Technologies Inc. was incorporated in Hsinchu City, Taiwan's semiconductor-cluster hub, entering the back-end (assembly and test) segment of the industry at a time when Taiwan's foundry/IDM ecosystem was rapidly scaling.

Early 2000s

Memory & Display-Driver Specialization

The company built out core competencies in memory (DRAM/Flash/SRAM) testing and assembly alongside display driver IC (DDIC) packaging for the then-booming LCD panel industry, establishing the dual-pillar business model (memory + display driver) that still defines ChipMOS today.

Mid-2000s

Bumping & Turnkey Capability Build-Out

ChipMOS added gold-bumping services — a precursor advanced-packaging technology — and turnkey assembly-and-test solutions, positioning itself as a vertically integrated, full-service back-end partner rather than a single-process subcontractor.

2014

Taiwan Stock Exchange Listing

ChipMOS listed its common shares on the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TWSE: 8150) in April 2014, complementing its pre-existing NASDAQ ADS listing (IMOS) and broadening its local-currency institutional shareholder base.

2020–2024

Automotive & Mixed-Signal Diversification

The company expanded into automotive and industrial display-driver and mixed-signal applications to diversify away from pure consumer-LCD cyclicality, a strategy that paid off as automotive/industrial grew to over 28% of Q2 2026 sales even as consumer-display demand softened.

2025

Display-Driver Downcycle & Margin Trough

2025 was a difficult transition year: display-driver assembly revenue fell from NT$7.32B (2024) to NT$5.87B on customer inventory corrections, with high fixed costs (61% of total cost of revenue in the DDIC business) compressing margins and triggering a Q2 2025 net loss of NT$533.1 million.

2026

AI-Memory Inflection & Tainan Expansion

An AI-driven memory demand/supply imbalance drove record monthly and quarterly revenues through H1 2026 (Q2 revenue +28.7% YoY, gross margin from 6.6% to 18.0%), prompting management to acquire a new Tainan facility and guide 2026-27 capex above 25% of revenue to add dedicated memory testing and turnkey assembly capacity from 2027.

03Value Chain

ChipMOS sits at the "back end" of the semiconductor value chain — after wafer fabrication, before the finished chip reaches an OEM's bill of materials.

Upstream — Wafer Supply

Fabless companies, IDMs and foundries (e.g., memory makers, display-driver designers) ship finished or near-finished wafers to ChipMOS for back-end processing; ChipMOS does not fabricate wafers itself, so its volumes are a direct derivative of upstream fab output and customer order patterns.

Bumping

For advanced-packaging-bound die, ChipMOS applies gold-bumping — depositing solder/gold bumps on wafer pads — a precursor process enabling flip-chip and other advanced interconnect packaging; currently the company's lowest-utilized process (65% in Q2 2026), representing latent capacity.

Assembly

Dicing, die-attach, wire-bonding/flip-chip, and encapsulation into leadframe- or substrate-based packages across a broad package portfolio — the segment running hottest (78% utilization in Q2 2026) on surging memory-module demand.

Testing

Wafer-level and final testing for memory, mixed-signal and display-driver semiconductors — critical quality-assurance step before chips ship to OEMs; test utilization stood at 74% in Q2 2026, with further automation investment underway to relieve bottlenecks.

ChipMOS's "turnkey" offering bundles bumping, assembly and test into a single outsourced relationship — a key differentiator versus single-process competitors and a driver of customer stickiness once a design is qualified on ChipMOS's lines.

04Customer Types

Fabless Memory & Logic Companies

Fabless DRAM/NAND/mixed-signal designers outsourcing 100% of their back-end needs — the fastest-growing customer cohort in 2026, driving the memory segment to 51% of revenue (+46% YoY) on DDR4/DDR5 scaling and data-center/AI demand.

Integrated Device Manufacturers (IDMs)

Larger IDMs that outsource overflow capacity or specific package/test steps they don't run in-house — a segment where ChipMOS's quality/reliability reputation and failure-analysis capability are key relationship differentiators.

Display-Driver & Automotive/Industrial Customers

LCD/OLED panel-driver chip designers, increasingly automotive- and industrial-grade; automotive alone made up over 39% of DDIC revenue in Q2 2026, a deliberate diversification away from pure consumer-electronics cyclicality.

Customer concentration is a genuine structural feature of the business: per the company's own 20-F disclosure, the top five customers accounted for 61% of revenue in 2025 — meaning order patterns, inventory cycles or competitive losses at even one or two large accounts can materially swing quarterly results.

07Porter's Five Forces

Threat of New Entrants LOW

OSAT is capital-intensive (ChipMOS itself is guiding 2026-27 capex above 25% of revenue just to expand existing lines) and requires years of process qualification with memory/display-driver customers. New greenfield entrants face a multi-year, multi-hundred-million-dollar qualification gauntlet, making genuine new entry rare — the real competitive threat is from existing scaled players, not newcomers.

Bargaining Power of Suppliers MEDIUM

Equipment suppliers (test handlers, wire bonders, bumping tools) are concentrated among a handful of global toolmakers, giving them meaningful pricing power, especially during the current industry-wide capex upcycle when lead times for advanced test/assembly equipment are stretched.

Bargaining Power of Buyers HIGH

With the top five customers representing 61% of 2025 revenue, ChipMOS's largest accounts hold real negotiating leverage over pricing and allocation — a risk that is currently masked by a favorable demand/supply imbalance (management notes it is raising prices because "demand continues to exceed capacity") but would re-emerge sharply in any downcycle.

Threat of Substitutes LOW

Outsourced assembly and test is a necessary, non-substitutable step for any fabless company and most IDMs; the only "substitute" is insourcing back-end capacity, which requires capital and expertise most fabless customers deliberately avoid — this is a structurally low-substitution-risk business.

Competitive Rivalry HIGH

Rivalry is intense and multi-front: ChipMOS competes against larger-scale generalist OSATs (ASE/SPIL) with superior advanced-packaging capability, memory-focused peers (Powertech) with potentially deeper customer relationships, and a direct DDIC rival (Chipbond) fighting the same display-driver downcycle. The company's own filings acknowledge many competitors have "more advanced technologies and greater financial and other resources" — ChipMOS's survival strategy is specialization and quality reputation rather than scale, which works well in an upcycle (current pricing power) but leaves it more exposed than larger peers in a downturn.

08Product Deep Dives

Memory Assembly & Test (51% of Q2 2026 Revenue)

DRAM, NAND/NOR Flash and SRAM assembly and testing — the current growth engine, with DRAM sales up over 70% YoY in Q2 2026 on DDR4 scaling and early DDR5 ramp. This is the direct AI-infrastructure-adjacent exposure: as hyperscalers and server OEMs scramble for memory capacity, ChipMOS's back-end bottleneck position has translated into genuine pricing power.

Display Driver IC (DDIC) Assembly & Test (~39% of Q2 2026 Revenue)

Bumping, assembly and test for LCD/OLED panel-driver chips. 2025 was a down year (revenue -19.8%) on customer inventory corrections, but automotive and OLED strength has supported a partial recovery in 2026 — automotive now over 39% of DDIC revenue, reflecting the strategic pivot away from pure consumer-panel cyclicality.

Bumping Services

Gold-bumping as a precursor advanced-packaging process; currently the lowest-utilized line (65% in Q2 2026), representing the most immediate source of incremental capacity should demand continue to run hot without requiring fresh capex.

Turnkey & Mixed-Signal/MEMS

Bundled bump-assemble-test turnkey solutions plus niche mixed-signal and MEMS testing — smaller in absolute revenue terms but strategically important for customer stickiness, since a turnkey relationship is far harder for a customer to unwind than a single-process contract.

10Management Commentary (Q2 2026 Earnings Call)

Management: Chairman S.J. Cheng; VP of Finance Silvia Su.

On the earnings inflection: Chairman Cheng stated, "We delivered a strong start to 2026 with a real earnings inflection," attributing the swing to profitability to demand exceeding capacity, improved pricing, and favorable operating leverage — gross margin expanded 11.4 percentage points YoY and operating margin rebounded from 0.4% to 12.8%.

On pricing power: Cheng noted explicitly that "demand continues to exceed capacity," which has allowed the company to raise prices on services tied to constrained resources — a rare moment of genuine pricing leverage for an OSAT provider historically seen as a price-taker.

On segment outlook: management guided that memory momentum should outpace driver-IC growth through H2 2026, with DRAM expected to see further Q3 expansion driven by continued DDR4 demand and DDR5 ramp-up; the automotive-panel and OLED-driven strength within DDIC (automotive >39% of that segment) was called out as a stabilizing offset to consumer-display softness.

On capital allocation: VP of Finance Silvia Su disclosed that 2026 capital spending is projected to exceed 25% of annual revenue, well above the company's long-term target of below 20%, with similarly elevated capex anticipated in 2027 as ChipMOS brings its newly acquired Tainan facility online to add dedicated memory testing and turnkey assembly capacity starting in 2027.

12Growth Levers

1. AI/Data-Center Memory Packaging Demand

DRAM sales +70% YoY in Q2 2026 on DDR4 scaling and DDR5 ramp, directly tied to hyperscaler and AI-server memory procurement — the single largest near-term growth driver, with management flagging demand still exceeding capacity as of the August 2026 monthly print.

2. Capacity Expansion (Tainan)

The new Tainan facility, online from 2027, specifically targets memory testing and turnkey assembly — directly monetizing the current capacity-constrained environment and extending ChipMOS's addressable memory-OSAT revenue pool.

3. Automotive/Industrial DDIC Diversification

Automotive now over 39% of the display-driver segment, helping offset consumer-LCD cyclicality with a structurally growing, higher-reliability-requirement end market less prone to the sharp inventory swings that hit consumer electronics.

4. Pricing Power in a Capacity-Constrained Market

For the first time in several years, ChipMOS has genuine pricing leverage ("demand continues to exceed capacity" per Chairman Cheng) — if sustained through 2027, this alone can structurally re-rate the company's margin profile even without further volume growth.

The combined effect of these levers is visible in the trajectory from Q2 2025 (net loss) to Q2 2026 (record profit) within four quarters — a genuine operating-leverage inflection rather than a one-off beat, though it is also a textbook semiconductor upcycle that has historically mean-reverted once capacity catches up with demand.

Key Trend

The defining story in ChipMOS's recent numbers is a textbook operating-leverage inflection: revenue grew a strong but not extraordinary 28.7% YoY in Q2 2026, yet gross margin nearly tripled (6.6% → 18.0%) and the company swung from a NT$533.1M net loss to a NT$891.7M net profit in the same four-quarter window. This is consistent with a largely fixed-cost manufacturing base (the 20-F itself notes DDIC fixed costs alone were 61% of total 2025 cost of revenue) where incremental utilization and pricing flow disproportionately to the bottom line — the mechanism is genuine, but it is symmetric: the same fixed-cost structure that is now driving explosive margin expansion on the way up drove the 2025 margin collapse on the way down, and will do so again whenever the current AI-memory demand surge normalizes.

15Key Risks

HIGH Semiconductor Cyclicality / Demand Normalization

The entire 2026 re-rating thesis rests on an AI-driven memory demand/supply imbalance that is, by definition, a temporary capacity-scarcity condition; as industry-wide capacity (including ChipMOS's own Tainan facility) comes online through 2027, pricing power and utilization-driven margin expansion could reverse sharply, as it did in 2025.

HIGH Customer Concentration

Top five customers represented 61% of 2025 revenue per the company's own 20-F disclosure — a structural vulnerability to any single large account's inventory correction, competitive loss, or insourcing decision, with no easy diversification fix given qualification lead times in OSAT relationships.

HIGH Taiwan Geopolitical Concentration

100% of ChipMOS's manufacturing capacity sits in Taiwan; any escalation in cross-strait tensions, export-control actions, or Taiwan-specific tariff measures on semiconductor content (explicitly flagged as a risk in the 20-F) could disrupt operations or prompt customers to diversify their supply chains away from Taiwan-based OSAT providers.

MEDIUM Display-Driver Segment Structural Weakness

DDIC assembly revenue fell from NT$7.32B (2024) to NT$5.87B (2025) on inventory corrections, and with fixed costs at 61% of DDIC cost of revenue, any renewed consumer-panel softness would disproportionately hit segment profitability even as memory booms.

MEDIUM Elevated Capex & Execution Risk

2026-27 capex guided above 25% of revenue (vs. a <20% long-term target) to fund the Tainan facility; any delay, cost overrun, or slower-than-expected ramp would pressure free cash flow and return ratios at a time when the stock is already priced for a smooth execution path.

MEDIUM Valuation / Momentum Risk

A ~4x rally off the 52-week low with a 14.6% single-day move on October 2, 2026 reflects extreme investor enthusiasm; any disappointment in the November 2026 Q3 print relative to elevated expectations could trigger a sharp multiple de-rating, independent of underlying business fundamentals.

Investment Summary

ChipMOS Technologies offers direct, differentiated exposure to the AI-driven memory demand/supply imbalance through its position as a specialized OSAT provider for DRAM, DDR5 and related memory packaging and testing. The Q2 2026 results mark a genuine operating-leverage inflection — gross margin nearly tripled YoY and the company swung from a net loss to a record profit — driven by capacity scarcity that has, for the first time in years, given ChipMOS real pricing power. Management's aggressive capex program (above 25% of revenue, funding the new Tainan facility from 2027) signals confidence that this demand is structural rather than transient, and the automotive/industrial diversification within the display-driver segment provides a partial hedge against consumer-electronics cyclicality.
