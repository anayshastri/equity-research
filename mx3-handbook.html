The core idea

One trade record, many readers

The trader, the risk manager, the settlements team and the accountant are all reading the same trade.

That is MX.3's strength, and its main source of cross-team breaks. The same record carries the front-office price, the risk sensitivities, the settlement instructions and the accounting entries. No team has a private copy, so a configuration change made for one team is felt by all of them.

Scope. This handbook is for people who already use MX.3 daily. It is not click-by-click screen training, and it does not replace your implementation's functional specifications. Terminology follows common MX.3 usage; your bank's version and configuration may name things differently.

Module 1

Platform and data model

What sits under the screens, and the static data every team depends on.

Client

Java GUI where users work in screens, simulations and viewers. Access rights are set per user group.

Application services

Core services and launchers host business processes, messaging (MxML Exchange) and batch processing.

Database

A relational database, commonly Oracle, holds trades, static data, market data and results.

Datamart

A separate reporting store filled by feeders. Most reports and downstream extracts read from here.

Interfaces

MxML (Murex XML) for trades and static data; SWIFT, FpML, market data feeds and GL interfaces.

Deployment

On-premise, private cloud, or MXSaaS (vendor-managed). MX.3 is certified on major public clouds.

Module 2

Front office and market data

Which market data is read by whom, and what happens between a price and a booked trade.

Live vs closing

Live feeds drive intraday pricing. The end-of-day closing snapshot is the official set for revaluation, P&L and accounting. Many P&L disputes start here.

Market data sets

Separate sets for FO, risk and accounting allow different sources or close times. That helps control, but only if someone documents who reads which set.

Curves

Multi-curve set-up: OIS/RFR curves for CSA discounting, projection curves per index and tenor, and basis between them. The curve instruments decide the risk buckets.

Volatility and credit

Vol surfaces and cubes, FX vol by delta, credit spread curves with recovery assumptions. A stale surface shows up as vega P&L that never moves.

Price is not booking

A template can override the conventions used when pricing. On disputes, compare the two.

Packages

Multi-leg structures book as linked contracts. An action on one leg may need to cascade to the others.

Back-dated trades

A trade entered today with a past trade date hits today's P&L as a new trade.

Pre-trade and execution

  1. Pre-trade pricing and checks

    Before TFront office
    In MX.3
    Trader prices the swap in a simulation on live curves; pre-deal checks run against credit and market limits (MLC), and incremental XVA is shown if configured.
    Output
    A quoted fixed rate and a limit decision.
    Control
    A limit breach routes to approval before the trade can be done.

  2. Execution and capture

    TFront office
    In MX.3
    The trade is booked from the INR swap generator, which fills calendars, day count, fixing source and payment lags. The trader enters notional, rate, tenor, portfolio and counterparty. Electronic trades arrive through an interface (MxML).
    Output
    A live contract with a trade ID and full cash flow schedule.
    Control
    Booking cut-off time; trade date vs entry date recorded.

  3. Enrichment and validation

    TMiddle office
    In MX.3
    The contract workflow moves the trade to Validated after checks on portfolio, typology, counterparty and conventions. Non-standard trades need a second approver.
    Output
    Validated status, which releases the downstream workflows.
    Control
    Four-eyes check; typology decides which workflows and accounting rules apply.

Post-trade processing

  1. Confirmation and matching

    T to T+1Operations
    In MX.3
    The exchange (documents) workflow generates the confirmation, sends it by platform or SWIFT (MT360/361), and matches the counterparty's version.
    Output
    Matched confirmation, or a break with the differing fields.
    Control
    Ageing of unmatched confirmations is reported and escalated.

  2. Clearing

    TOperations
    In MX.3
    The trade is submitted to the CCP (CCIL for INR interbank swaps). On acceptance, it is novated: the CCP becomes the counterparty and the original bilateral trade is replaced.
    Output
    A cleared trade facing the CCP, with margin requirements.
    Control
    Reconcile MX.3 cleared trades to the CCP's report daily.

  3. Regulatory trade reporting

    T to T+1Operations / Compliance
    In MX.3
    Trade data is reported to the trade repository (CCIL for RBI reporting of INR OTC derivatives) directly or through an extract.
    Output
    Acknowledged report with a unique trade identifier.
    Control
    Rejections and late reports are tracked to closure.

Daily, for the life of the trade

  1. End-of-day revaluation and P&L

    Every business dayProduct control
    In MX.3
    The EOD batch revalues the swap on the closing curve. Booked at market, the swap is worth close to zero on day one. After rates rise, the batch produces a value like the ₹62.3 lakh in the next section.
    Output
    Official MTM, daily P&L and P&L explain.
    Control
    Independent price verification of curve inputs; unexplained P&L above tolerance investigated.

  2. Risk measurement and limits

    Every business dayMarket and credit risk
    In MX.3
    Sensitivities (DV01 by tenor bucket, about ₹2.6 lakh per basis point for this trade), VaR and stress, exposure and limit usage.
    Output
    Risk reports and limit utilisation.
    Control
    Breaches trigger approval workflows.

  3. Margin and collateral

    Every business dayCollateral
    In MX.3
    Variation margin follows the change in MTM. For a cleared trade, the CCP calls margin; for a bilateral trade, the CSA terms (threshold, minimum transfer amount) drive the call.
    Output
    Margin call issued or received, then settled.
    Control
    Disputes above threshold are escalated and investigated.

  4. Accounting

    Every business dayFinance
    In MX.3
    Events (revaluation, accrual) create entries through accounting rules selected by typology, portfolio and entity. Entries are aggregated and sent to the GL.
    Output
    Subledger entries and the GL feed.
    Control
    Subledger to GL reconciliation; P&L to FO reconciliation.

Periodic events

  1. Fixing and reset

    Each periodMarket data / FO support
    In MX.3
    The floating rate is fixed from the published benchmark and applied to the cash flow. Year 1 in the example fixes at 6.40%.
    Output
    A known floating amount for the period.
    Control
    A missing fixing blocks the EOD batch and the payment.

  2. Coupon settlement

    Each payment dateOperations
    In MX.3
    The deliverables workflow nets the two legs and produces a payment. Year 1: fixed ₹6.50 crore against floating ₹6.40 crore, so the bank pays a net ₹10 lakh.
    Output
    Payment instruction released; nostro updated.
    Control
    Nostro reconciliation; failed payments chased.

  3. Lifecycle events

    As neededFO, Ops, Finance
    In MX.3
    Amendments, partial unwinds, novations and portfolio compression go through the event workflow. Each can restart confirmation, rebook accounting and change risk.
    Output
    An amended or reduced contract with an audit trail.
    Control
    Back-dated events are reviewed for P&L and accounting impact.

End of life

  1. Maturity or early termination

    T + 3 years, or earlierAll teams
    In MX.3
    The final coupon settles, or an unwind fee is agreed and settled. Unrealised MTM moves to realised P&L; margin is returned.
    Output
    Contract status Matured or Terminated; no future cash flows.
    Control
    Every workflow closed; no residual balances in the subledger.

  2. Archive and retention

    After maturityIT / Operations
    In MX.3
    Dead trades are purged from live processing by the archiving policy and kept for audit and regulatory retention.
    Output
    A smaller live database; history still available for audit.
    Control
    Retention periods agreed with compliance.

Worked example

Valuing a simple interest rate swap

The same fictional trade, booked at 6.50% when that was the market rate and valued later, after rates have risen. Move the slider to shift the curve and watch the value and the legs change.

A swap is two streams of cash flows. Its value to the bank is the present value of what it receives minus the present value of what it pays:

MTM (payer) = PV(floating leg received) − PV(fixed leg paid) DF(t) = 1 / (1 + z(t))^t discount factor from zero rate z Forward(t) = DF(t−1) / DF(t) − 1 implied floating rate for period t PV floating = Σ Notional × Forward(t) × DF(t) (equals Notional × (1 − DF(3))) PV fixed = Σ Notional × 6.50% × DF(t) Par rate = (1 − DF(3)) / Σ DF(t) the fixed rate that makes MTM zero

Base curve (fictional zero rates): 1Y 6.40%, 2Y 6.60%, 3Y 6.75%. Annual compounding, whole-year periods.

Reading the result

At the base curve the par rate is about 6.74%, above the contract's 6.50%. The bank locked in paying 6.50% when the market now charges about 6.74% for the same swap, so the position is worth about ₹62.3 lakh to the bank. A quick check: the rate gap of roughly 23.6 bp times an annuity of about ₹2.64 lakh per bp gives the same figure.

Move the slider right (rates up) and the MTM rises. A payer swap gains when rates rise, which is what a positive DV01 of about ₹2.6 lakh per bp says. Move it down about 25 bp and the MTM falls close to zero, because the par rate falls back towards 6.50%.

Where each number lives in MX.3

The zero rates come from the closing INR curve in the market data set. The generator supplies the schedule and conventions. The EOD batch computes the MTM and the leg PVs, risk computes DV01 by tenor bucket, and accounting books the MTM (see the entries in the finance section). Simplified on purpose. A real INR MIBOR OIS compounds the overnight rate daily through each period, uses actual day counts and payment lags from the generator, and discounts on the collateral curve. The logic is the same; the arithmetic has more steps. All figures here are fictional.

Module 3

Risk, limits and collateral

Their quality depends on data owned by the front office and static data teams.

Sensitivities

Delta and DV01 by curve and tenor bucket, gamma, vega by expiry and strike, FX delta, credit spread 01. Change a curve instrument and the risk report changes shape.

VaR and stress

Historical and Monte Carlo VaR, expected shortfall and stress scenarios. Full revaluation against sensitivity-based methods trades speed for accuracy.

FRTB

Standardised approach (sensitivities-based method, default risk charge, residual risk add-on) and internal models. The P&L attribution test compares FO and risk P&L, so data drift between them can fail a desk.

Counterparty risk

PFE across netting sets, CVA, DVA, FVA, MVA and KVA, and SA-CCR for capital. Netting sets are static data: set them up wrong and exposure is overstated.

Limits (MLC)

Murex Limits Controller checks credit, market and settlement limits pre- and post-deal. Breaches go to approval workflows.

Collateral

CSA terms, then exposure, then margin call, then agree or dispute, then settle. Initial margin under the uncleared margin rules uses ISDA SIMM sensitivities, so poor sensitivities give wrong calls.

Where breaks come from

Static data

Missing SSI, wrong legal entity, outdated netting agreement. Start with the counterparty set-up.

Market data

Missing fixing, stale curve, wrong fixing source. Start with the EOD market data log.

Configuration

Typology mapped to the wrong workflow or accounting rule.

Timing

Trades after the EOD cut-off; back-dated amendments.

Interfaces

MxML rejects, confirmation platform mismatches.

Manual overrides

Forced status changes that skip a workflow step. Check the audit trail.

Module 6

Asset classes

What changes processing in each one, and where it tends to break.

Rates and IRD

  • IRS, OIS, basis, FRAs, cross-currency, caps, floors, swaptions, bonds, repo
  • Set-up: RFR compounding (lookback, observation shift, lockout); multi-curve with CSA discounting; clearing at LCH, CME, CCIL
  • Breaks: late or missing fixings; wrong bond static; repo collateral substitutions

FX and money markets

  • Spot, forwards, FX swaps, NDFs, vanilla and barrier options, deposits, call money, CDs and CP
  • Set-up: value-date calendars for both currencies plus USD; forward points; NDF fixing source; CLS eligibility
  • Breaks: holiday mismatch gives a wrong value date; NDF fixing not captured; swap legs amended separately

Credit and equity

  • Single-name and index CDS, TRS, equity options and swaps
  • Set-up: ISDA standard CDS model, standard coupons, IMM dates; recovery; reference entity static; dividends and corporate actions
  • Breaks: credit events and successions; index rolls; corporate actions not applied

Commodities

  • Futures, average-price swaps, Asian options, precious metals, energy
  • Set-up: price source per index; averaging period and pricing calendar; units; delivery locations
  • Breaks: averaging over holidays; unit or lot mismatches; physical and financial settlement mixed up

India set-up points. MIBOR OIS is the main INR swap; Modified MIFOR replaced MIFOR after LIBOR ended. CCIL clears INR swaps and USD/INR forwards and runs the trade repository. Indian banks may trade INR NDFs since 2020. Check each point against current RBI directions.

Module 7

Exercises

Work in mixed groups with at least one person from FO, risk, operations and finance. Ten minutes each. Open the model answer only after discussing.

1. The missing fixing

It is 7 pm. The EOD batch stops at fixings: today's floating-rate fixing was not loaded because the feed changed its instrument code. 40 trades reset today.

  1. Which EOD steps are blocked, in what order?
  2. If the batch is forced through with yesterday's fixing, what does each team see tomorrow?
  3. Who owns the fix, and who signs off the rerun?
Model answer

Steps 4 to 8 are blocked. Forcing with a stale fixing gives wrong cash flows, wrong accruals and entries that must be reversed, a fixing effect in tomorrow's P&L explain, and wrong settlement amounts if a payment is near.

Market data owns the feed mapping; product control and EOD support usually sign off the rerun. A correct delay beats a forced wrong run.

2. Amendment after confirmation

An FX swap's far-leg rate is amended on T+2, after both legs were confirmed and the near leg has settled.

  1. Which workflows restart, and which must not?
  2. What accounting reverses, and on which date?
  3. How is the counterparty told, and with what message?
Model answer

The event workflow and the far-leg confirmation restart. The settled near leg must not be touched or re-settled.

Far-leg MTM and forward-points accrual reverse and rebook; the date depends on whether amendments are booked as of today or back-dated, which drives the P&L explain. The counterparty receives an amended MT300 (or a cancel and new) for the far leg, and matching restarts.

3. Unexplained P&L

The rates desk shows ₹2.4 crore of unexplained P&L (fictional) on a quiet day. Curves moved less than half a basis point.

  1. List the first five things you check, in order.
  2. Which show in the datamart, and which only on screen?
  3. What control stops it happening again?
Model answer

A sensible order: new and back-dated trades; amendments and cancellations; fixings applied today; the market data set used (complete snapshot, no stale points); static or template changes such as a changed generator convention. Only then suspect the model.

The datamart shows trade population, P&L components and fixings from the feeder run; audit trail and configuration changes usually need the GUI or change logs. Prevention: change control on static and market data configuration, with a P&L impact check before release.
