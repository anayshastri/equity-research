01

Company Overview

Deep Industries Ltd is a Vadodara-based oil & gas support-services provider — describing itself as India's only integrated onshore-and-offshore oilfield-services company. From its origins as a niche natural-gas compression specialist (incorporated 2006 as Deep CH4), it has built out an integrated platform spanning gas compression & dehydration, drilling, workover, well testing, and production enhancement (PEC) — serving ONGC, GSPC, Oil India and private producers, and now expanding offshore (via the Dolphin Offshore Shipping acquisition) and overseas (UAE).

The investment story is a transformation-plus-visibility story. FY26 was operationally one of the strongest years in the company's history — revenue +55% to ₹890.7 Cr, EBITDA +61% to ₹424.8 Cr at a ~48% margin — but reported profit was clouded by a large non-cash write-off of legacy receivables (from the Kandla acquisition), which makes headline PAT and P/E misleading. Q1 FY27 stripped that noise away: PAT +45% to ₹85.4 Cr, rebounding sharply from a one-off-driven Q4 loss. With an order book above ₹3,000 Cr (~3.4x FY26 revenue) and management guiding 25–30% revenue growth, the underlying engine looks strong; the debate is execution, receivables clean-up, and valuation.

Business Model & Service Lines

Deep Industries is an asset-based oilfield-services contractor: it owns specialised equipment (compressors, rigs, offshore vessels) and deploys it on multi-year contracts to oil & gas producers, earning day-rate / contract revenue at high margins. Crucially, as a service provider it benefits from exploration & production activity — capex and drilling intensity — rather than directly from crude prices, giving it a somewhat different risk profile from producers.

Why the model earns ~48% EBITDA margins
Deep's very high EBITDA margin (~48% in FY26) reflects an equipment-rental / specialised-services model: once the compressor, rig or vessel is deployed on a contract, incremental revenue drops heavily to EBITDA. The Production Enhancement Contract (PEC) model — where Deep takes on an oilfield (e.g. Rajahmundry) and is paid on incremental production — is a higher-value evolution: management sees >40% margins and revenue scaling from ~₹70 Cr (FY26) toward ~₹140 Cr (FY27) on that one contract, and it's a first-of-its-kind ONGC template that could open more such awards. The offshore tug already runs at ~50% margins on USD 17–20k/day. High margins + long contracts = the core attraction; the offset is high capital intensity to grow (rigs and vessels are expensive).

The FY26 Write-Off, Dissected

The single most important thing to understand about FY26
FY26's headline profit is distorted by a ₹208.28 Cr (₹20,828.49 lakh) non-cash exceptional write-off of legacy trade receivables tied to the Kandla Energy & Chemicals acquisition/merger. This is a one-time, non-cash accounting charge — not an operating loss. Reported PAT and any trailing P/E built on it are therefore misleading — some vendors even show a negative trailing P/E. The right lens is EBITDA and cash / normalised earnings, which is exactly what Q1 FY27 (PAT ₹85.4 Cr, clean quarter) reveals once the one-off is behind.

Deep runs a high-margin, capital-intensive model: ~48% EBITDA margins on services, with growth requiring investment in rigs and, increasingly, offshore vessels. The offshore push — kicked off by the Dolphin acquisition and an already-operating tug at ~50% margins — is backed by planned ₹400–500 Cr of capex in PSVs (platform supply vessels), DSVs (diving support vessels) and tugs to scale the offshore footprint.

Offshore is the growth optionality — and the execution risk
The offshore expansion is the most exciting and most uncertain part of the story. Positive: offshore services command high day-rates and margins (the tug already earns ~50%), the Dolphin acquisition gave a running start, and India's offshore E&P (ONGC's deepwater, etc.) is a large, under-served market — so ₹400–500 Cr of vessel capex could add a materially higher-margin, higher-scale revenue stream. Risk: offshore is capital-heavy, operationally complex, and new to Deep; vessel acquisition/utilisation, execution and financing must all land. Funding ₹400–500 Cr capex while keeping the balance sheet clean (D/E is currently low ~0.10) is manageable but must be watched. The offshore ramp is the swing factor for medium-term growth and returns.

The metric that decides earnings quality: cash conversion
For a high-margin services company with a messy receivables history, OCF-to-EBITDA conversion and debtor days are the truest quality tests — they show whether the ~48% EBITDA margin is real cash or accounting profit trapped in slow-paying receivables. FY26's ₹218 Cr OCF (growing despite the write-off noise) is reassuring; the goal is falling debtor days as legacy issues resolve and the client base diversifies away from slow-paying PSU concentration. If cash conversion improves alongside the strong order book and offshore ramp, Deep is a genuinely high-quality compounder; if receivables stay bloated (or the Dolphin arbitration goes badly), the quality — and multiple — stay capped. Watch OCF/EBITDA, debtor days, net debt, and the arbitration outcome each quarter.
