Executive Summary — Where Is Institutional Money Flowing?

The big picture
The recovery that began two weeks ago kept extending, but the driver shifted from FII flows to commodities. Nifty 50 gained a further 0.77% this week — a third straight positive week — with breadth improving for a third consecutive week (959 advancers vs 595 decliners, median stock +1.17%). Unlike the prior two weeks, though, the dominant catalyst wasn't institutional cash flow (FII was roughly flat, DII solidly positive but unremarkable) — it was crude oil's sharp Monday collapse on US-Iran diplomatic developments, which lifted the whole market in one session and set the tone for the week.

The Iran/Hormuz story is now the single biggest swing factor in this report — more than FII/DII flows, more than the RBI. Brent fell 8% in a single session on Monday's talks, then gave back some of that as tensions resurfaced Wednesday-Friday. This is now showing up across three separate sections: the rupee (strengthened on lower oil, then pared gains), the debt market (RBI's dovish stance was reinforced by softer crude easing inflation worries), and obviously equities directly. Anyone tracking this report weekly should treat Middle-East headline risk as the dominant macro variable right now, ahead of the usual FII-flow narrative.

Within India, DMA-based breadth confirms the trend is broadening, not just the headline index: stocks above their 200-day average rose across all four cap buckets for a third straight week (Large 66%→69%, Small 61.6%→70.0% — the biggest single-bucket jump), and this week's first-ever DMA50 crossover data shows 196 stocks crossing above their 50-day average against 108 dropping below — a net +88, further confirming broad-based improvement rather than a narrow rally. See Section 10 for the full breakdown.

One data-quality note for you: this week's US stocks export lost its Market Cap and Stock Price columns (present last week), and this week's FII/DII, derivatives OI, and most commodity figures had more search gaps than usual — flagging in case the underlying source changed something on their end.

What to watch next week
Whether the Iran/Hormuz diplomatic track continues to de-escalate (bullish for equities, bonds, and the rupee simultaneously) or flares up again; whether FII flows turn more decisively positive now that the rupee has stabilized; and whether the breadth-broadening trend (3 weeks running now) continues into a 4th week once the rolling comparison window is fully populated.
