01

Company Overview

Macpower CNC Machines Ltd (NSE: MACPOWER / BSE: 535057) is a high-quality, founder-led, net-debt-free manufacturer of Computerized Numerical Control (CNC) machine tools based in Metoda GIDC, Rajkot (Gujarat) — incorporated in 2003 and IPO'd in December 2018. It is one of India's leading domestic CNC machine-tool makers, offering 380+ models across turning centres, vertical machining centres (VMCs), horizontal machining centres (HMCs), vertical turret lathes (VTLs), grinders and turn-mill centres — spanning 27+ product segments, 3,000 application solutions and 13,389+ installations — and is recognised as being among the lowest-cost producers in its industry. Its machines serve auto/auto-ancillary, bearings, valves, pumps, tool-room, agri, textile and piping customers, with a growing push into defence, aerospace, power and railways. Promoter (Doshi family) holding is a high ~73.2% with zero pledge.

The investment story is a structural domestic-manufacturing growth play with a premiumization angle. Macpower rides India's Make-in-India, import-substitution, capex-revival and China+1 tailwinds: as Indian manufacturing scales, demand for domestically-made, cost-competitive CNC machine tools grows, and Macpower — a low-cost, wide-portfolio domestic supplier — is well-placed to take share from imports (historically Taiwanese/Chinese/European). It has compounded revenue at a ~26% five-year CAGR (well above the ~12% industry), and just delivered a record FY26: revenue ₹333.18 Cr (+27.2%), PAT ₹33.87 Cr (+33.1%), highest-ever on every line. It is premiumizing via its NEXA range (higher-value machines, now ~40% of the ~₹406 Cr order book), expanding capacity (2,500 machines/yr; a new 13-acre Rajkot lease with ₹50 Cr investment), and deepening its defence/aerospace presence — guiding 28-30% growth for FY27. The counterweight (explored in §14): it is a cyclical capital-goods business (demand tracks manufacturing capex), richly valued (~40-45x P/E), facing input-cost/margin pressure and strong competition (notably the much-larger Jyoti CNC), with a thin free float and unusually divergent vendor valuation data.

A cost-leadership + breadth + service moat — real, but not impregnable
Macpower's competitive edge is a combination of cost leadership, portfolio breadth, and domestic service/proximity — a solid, if not fortress-like, moat. Its strengths: (1) low-cost manufacturing — being among the lowest-cost producers lets it undercut imports and compete hard on price, the key lever for import substitution as Indian manufacturers seek affordable domestic CNC machines; (2) portfolio breadth — 380+ models across 27+ segments and 3,000 applications makes it a one-stop domestic supplier able to serve diverse industries and cross-sell; (3) a large installed base (13,389+ machines) that drives reference sales, service revenue and repeat/replacement demand, plus local service/support that imported machines can't easily match; and (4) an emerging premiumization/technology angle (the NEXA range, defence/aerospace qualification) that can lift value-add and stickiness. This underpins its above-industry growth. The honest limits of the moat (see §14): (1) machine tools are a competitive, somewhat commoditised, cyclical industry — cost leadership helps but doesn't confer pricing power the way a specialty niche does; (2) it faces a much larger, better-capitalised, technology-strong rival in Jyoti CNC (roughly ten times its market cap) plus Lakshmi Machine Works and global OEMs, which can out-invest it in R&D and higher-end machines; and (3) at the high-precision/high-value end, imported and premium machines still lead — Macpower's cost edge is strongest in the value segment. Net: a genuine, defensible position built on cost, breadth and service in a growing domestic market — but a competitive, cyclical industry where scale, technology and premiumization (NEXA, defence) will determine whether it can defend margins and keep taking share. A good moat for its niche; not an unassailable one.

FY26 showed genuine margin expansion at the net level (PAT +33.1% outpacing revenue +27.2%), and the zero-pledge promoter declaration and unmodified audit opinion reinforce clean governance. There is no sign of aggressive accounting, one-off dependence, or financial engineering — the growth is real product-driven growth. The only nuances (examined below) are that the level of earnings is cyclical and the operating (EBITDA) margin is under input-cost pressure — but those concern cyclicality and cost management, not the integrity or quality of the earnings. On the narrow question "are these clean, cash-backed, operating earnings?", the answer is a clear yes. Grade: green — clean, operating-led, cash-backed, dividend-paying earnings.

14.2 · Growth durability & cyclicality

Strong secular growth, but genuinely cyclical — don't extrapolate the peak
A defining characteristic to weigh is cyclicality. Macpower's ~26% five-year revenue CAGR is genuinely strong and above-industry, reflecting real share gains — but machine-tool demand is fundamentally capex-cyclical: it tracks manufacturers' investment in new capacity, which ebbs and flows with the economic and industrial cycle. The evidence is in Macpower's own numbers: FY25 revenue growth slowed to ~8.6% (below its 5-year CAGR) before FY26 rebounded to +27.2% — illustrating that growth is lumpy and cycle-dependent, not linear. The forensic implications: (1) the ~28-30% FY27 guidance and recent record growth should not be blindly extrapolated — they reflect a supportive capex environment that can moderate; (2) a manufacturing/capex slowdown would soften orders across Macpower's diversified-but-all-cyclical end-markets, and operating leverage would work in reverse; and (3) at a rich multiple, the valuation implicitly assumes the strong growth continues — a cyclical downturn would hit both earnings and the multiple. The mitigants are real: the structural Make-in-India/import-substitution tailwind provides a secular growth floor above pure cyclicality, the diversified end-markets smooth some volatility, and the debt-free balance sheet lets it ride out troughs. Net: a genuinely strong secular grower that is nonetheless cyclical — own it with realistic, through-cycle growth expectations rather than extrapolating peak-cycle rates, and watch the capex environment. Grade: amber — strong secular growth with real cyclicality; don't capitalise the peak.

14.3 · EBITDA-margin pressure

The key operating flag: rising input costs are squeezing operating margins
A specific, management-flagged concern is EBITDA-margin pressure from rising input and operating costs. While FY26 showed net-margin expansion, at the operating level the picture is more pressured: Q4 FY26 EBITDA grew only ~13% versus revenue growth of ~25%, indicating that cost inflation (raw materials, components, operating expenses) is compressing operating margins even as the company scales — and management has explicitly cited this as a focus, being questioned on how it plans to "restore EBITDA margins." This is a meaningful flag because: (1) the premiumization thesis is substantially a margin thesis — NEXA and defence are supposed to lift operating margins, so if EBITDA margins are instead being squeezed, the mix-shift isn't yet offsetting cost inflation; (2) in a cost-competitive, price-sensitive market (against a larger rival and imports), Macpower has limited pricing power to fully pass on input costs; and (3) a rich valuation assumes margin stability or expansion, so continued operating-margin pressure directly undermines the earnings-growth case. The forensic task: watch EBITDA margins closely — does premiumization + scale + cost management restore/expand them (validating the quality/mix thesis), or do rising costs keep squeezing them (signalling more commoditised, lower-quality growth)? This is arguably the single most important operating metric to monitor. Grade: amber — a genuine, management-acknowledged operating-margin squeeze; premiumization must prove it can offset input-cost inflation.

14.4 · Working capital & cash conversion

Machine-tool growth is working-capital-intensive — verify cash conversion
A standard but important flag for a fast-growing capital-goods manufacturer is working-capital intensity and cash conversion. Building and selling CNC machines requires significant inventory (components, work-in-progress, finished machines) and often extends receivables to customers — so rapid revenue growth typically consumes working capital, meaning reported profit may not fully convert to operating cash flow in high-growth phases. While Macpower's net-debt-free balance sheet indicates disciplined overall financial management (it hasn't needed to lever up to fund growth, a positive signal on cash generation), investors should still verify the cash-conversion picture as growth accelerates toward the guided 28-30%: (1) track operating cash flow vs PAT — is profit converting to cash, or being absorbed by inventory/receivables? (2) monitor the cash-conversion cycle (days inventory + receivables − payables) — is it stable or stretching? and (3) ensure the self-funded capacity expansion (₹50 Cr) plus working-capital needs don't outstrip internal generation and pressure the balance sheet. This is not currently a red flag — the debt-free status is reassuring and there's no evidence of a problem — but it is a structural characteristic of the business model that warrants routine monitoring, especially in an acceleration phase. Grade: amber — inherent working-capital intensity; verify cash conversion stays healthy as growth accelerates (debt-free status is reassuring).

14.5 · Competition vs a much larger rival

A ~10x-larger, better-capitalised rival dominates the premium end
A key competitive flag is Macpower's position relative to a much larger, stronger rival. Jyoti CNC Automation is roughly ten times Macpower's market capitalisation, executes far greater volumes at higher average realisations (~₹35.6 lakh/machine), and dominates the higher-value, more-technologically-advanced end of the CNC market — giving it superior R&D resources, scale economies, brand and pricing power. This matters for Macpower's thesis: (1) as Macpower tries to move up the value curve (NEXA, defence), it enters Jyoti's stronghold, where it faces a better-resourced incumbent — making margin/share gains harder to win; (2) Jyoti (and imports) can out-invest Macpower in technology and capacity, potentially capping its premium ambitions; and (3) Macpower's edge (cost leadership) is strongest in the value segment, which is also the most price-competitive and margin-pressured. The mitigants: Macpower is genuinely gaining share (~26% growth vs ~12% industry), the overall market is growing fast enough for multiple winners, and its cost/breadth/service model is well-suited to the large value segment. But investors should be realistic that Macpower is the smaller player in a field with a dominant premium rival, and that its up-market and margin ambitions face formidable competition. Grade: amber — a real, share-gaining challenger, but operating below a much larger, better-capitalised rival; premium ambitions face tough competition.

14.6 · Free float, data divergence & valuation

A thin float, wildly divergent data, and a rich multiple on a cyclical small-cap
The final cluster of flags — and the sharpest in valuation terms — concerns float, data quality and price. On free float & liquidity: with ~73% promoter holding and a small absolute share count, only ~27% floats, giving thin liquidity, limited institutional ownership, and high volatility on small volumes — a genuine risk to the stock (hard to trade in size, prone to sharp swings). On data divergence: this manifests concretely in unusually wide, conflicting vendor price/mcap figures (CMP quoted anywhere from ~₹890 to ~₹1,900+, mcap ~₹970-1,750 Cr, P/E ~29-45x across sources/dates) — a data-quality and price-discovery caveat that complicates precise valuation and reflects the thin float. On valuation: even taking the conservative end, Macpower trades at a rich ~40-45x trailing earnings and ~8-10x book — a full multiple for a cyclical, small-cap capital-goods maker, applied near what may be a cyclical-peak growth rate. This prices in continued high growth and margin resilience, leaving limited margin of safety: if growth normalises (cyclicality), margins stay pressured (input costs), or competition bites, the stock is vulnerable to de-rating — and the thin float would amplify the downside move. The forensic implication: respect the low-float liquidity/volatility risk in sizing, verify the true current price/valuation against live NSE/BSE data (given the divergence), and recognise that a rich multiple on a cyclical small-cap near peak growth demands discipline on entry and realistic through-cycle expectations. Grade: red — a rich multiple on a cyclical small-cap, with thin float and divergent data amplifying the valuation risk.
