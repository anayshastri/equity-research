01

Company Overview

Palantir Technologies Inc. (NASDAQ: PLTR) is a category-defining AI & data-analytics software company headquartered in Denver, Colorado — founded in 2003 and public since September 2020. It builds software platforms that let large, complex organisations integrate their data and act on it: Gotham (government/defence/intelligence), Foundry (commercial enterprise), Apollo (continuous-deployment infrastructure), and — the current growth engine — AIP (the Artificial Intelligence Platform), which operationalises large language models on an organisation's proprietary data. It is founder-led (CEO Alex Karp, with co-founders incl. Peter Thiel), GAAP-profitable, and holds ~$8B of cash and Treasuries with no debt.

The investment story is a rare "exceptional business at an extreme valuation" case — and the key news is that, far from decelerating, growth is re-accelerating. In Q2 2026 (reported 3 Aug 2026), Palantir grew revenue 93% YoY to $1.935B — its fastest-ever — driven by an explosive +149% in U.S. commercial revenue (as enterprises move AIP from pilots into production) and a durable +90% in U.S. government; it posted a 62% adjusted operating margin, GAAP net income of $1.06B (55% margin), 84% gross margin, and a record "Rule of 40" score of 155% (growth + margin) with net-dollar retention of 157%. Management raised FY2026 guidance to ~$8.15-8.16B (~82% growth), and CEO Karp framed the demand for "AI sovereignty" as lasting "at least another 18 months." On virtually every operating metric — growth, profitability, cash generation, retention, deal momentum — this is one of the best software franchises in the market. The single, decisive counterweight (§14) is valuation: at ~100x+ trailing sales and ~140x earnings, PLTR trades at a multiple with almost no precedent for a company of its scale, pricing in years of continued hyper-growth and leaving little margin of safety — as the stock's ~40% peak-to-trough fall earlier in 2026 (before the Q2 surge) demonstrated. Secondary risks: ~81% revenue concentration in the U.S., softer international growth, government-contract/political sensitivity (e.g. a UK NHS contract review), and rising AI-software competition.

Forensic Analysis

Putting on the forensic hat: Palantir is an unusual forensic subject — not because the business is questionable (it is genuinely exceptional and GAAP-profitable with a fortress balance sheet), but because almost the entire risk sits in one place: valuation. A conventional forensic screen looks for earnings-quality, leverage, cash-conversion or governance red flags — and on those, Palantir is remarkably clean (real GAAP profits, ~$8B cash, no debt, elite margins, strong cash flow, 157% retention). So this section scrutinises the things that actually matter for a stock like this: the extreme valuation and what it prices in, revenue concentration, competition, stock-based-compensation/dilution, growth-durability, and the extraordinary volatility. 14.4 · Stock-based compensation & dilution
